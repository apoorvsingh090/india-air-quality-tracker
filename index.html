Transparency

Sources, standards & limitations

This tracker is open data, rebuilt every hour by a scheduled GitHub Action. It is an information tool, not a substitute for official health advisories.

How the AQI is calculated

We use the CPCB National Air Quality Index (2014). For each pollutant a sub-index is interpolated from the official breakpoints, and the city AQI is the maximum sub-index. The headline value is the official CPCB daily bulletin: a 24-hour average ending 4 PM IST.

  • PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, NH3: 24-hour averages
  • CO, O3: 8-hour averages
  • Categories: Good, Satisfactory, Moderate, Poor, Very Poor, Severe

What the three sources are

CPCB bulletin — the regulator's official city AQI, published once a day. Station cross-checks — CPCB/SPCB CAAQMS feeds aggregated by AQICN/WAQI, used only when the feed is current (≤6 hours old). CAMS — the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service model via Open-Meteo, which gives hourly modelled concentrations for every city.

Limitations

AQI is a 24-hour city average and smooths short peaks, including firecracker bursts. Monitoring coverage varies; model values are estimates, not measurements. Official values should always take precedence for health decisions.

Diwali methodology

The Diwali-night value comes from the CPCB bulletin dated D+1, because its averaging window runs from 4 PM on Diwali to 4 PM the next day — capturing the evening and overnight fireworks. The baseline is the mean of the seven daily bulletins before Diwali, so each city is compared with itself rather than with a national average.

Update cadence

  • CPCB bulletin: once daily, around 4–6 PM IST
  • Station cross-checks + CAMS: hourly via GitHub Actions
  • Diwali study: recalculated every hour; finalised when D+1 bulletin appears

Health guidance

Above 100, sensitive groups should limit prolonged outdoor exertion. Above 200, everyone should reduce outdoor activity; above 300, avoid it. Use an N95 mask outdoors and keep windows closed during severe episodes.
